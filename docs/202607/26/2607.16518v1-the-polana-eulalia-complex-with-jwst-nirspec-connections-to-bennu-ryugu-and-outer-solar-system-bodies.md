## 摘要
富含碳的[C型复合]小行星提供了对早期太阳系的洞察，而C型复合小行星家族则是了解星子内部结构和原行星盘物质的可靠样本。本研究通过分析JWST NIRSpec数据，调查了波拉纳-尤拉莉亚复合体（PEC）内小行星的组成。这些数据是主带小行星3微米区域光谱分析（SAMBA3）项目的一部分。NIRSpec观测了尤拉莉亚家族的同名小行星[495]尤拉莉亚，以及波拉纳家族的三名成员：[2441]希布斯、[6712]霍恩斯坦和[6769]布罗科夫。对2.7微米区域的分析表明，PEC小行星表现出与富镁层状硅酸盐相关的吸收特征，这反过来强调了PEC两个家族过去都经历过高但非完全的水蚀变。尽管成分一致，[142]波拉纳和[495]尤拉莉亚的2.7微米波段比波拉纳家族小行星更深，表明大型和小型PEC小行星之间的水蚀变程度略有差异。3.0至4.0微米的微弱吸收表明，[495]尤拉莉亚的表面可能比[142]波拉纳含有更多的碳酸盐，这可能突出了每个家族形成和演化之间的细微差异。此外，PEC小行星还显示出与贝努和龙宫的光谱一致性，强化了它们在PEC中的共同起源。PEC小行星光谱中的微弱吸收也与主带内外的小天体有关，为PEC母体在外太阳系形成提供了进一步证据。

## Abstract
Carbon rich [C complex] asteroids provide insight into the early Solar System, and families of C complex asteroids serve as robust samples for learning about the internal structure of planetesimals and the materials of the protoplanetary disk. This work investigates the compositions of asteroids within the Polana Eulalia Complex [PEC] through analyses of JWST NIRSpec. Obtained as part of the Spectral Analysis of Main Belt Asteroids in the 3 um Region [SAMBA3] project. NIRSpec observations were conducted for the namesake asteroid of the Eulalia family, [495] Eulalia, as well as three members from the Polana family: [2441] Hibbs, [6712] Hornstein, and [6769] Brokoff. Analyses of the 2.7 um region demonstrate that PEC asteroids exhibit absorptions associated with Mg rich phyllosilicates, in turn underscoring high, though incomplete, degrees of aqueous alteration in the past for both PEC families. Although consistent in regard to composition, [142] Polana and [495] Eulalia show deeper 2.7 um bands than the Polana family asteroids, suggesting slight differences in the degrees of aqueous alteration between large and small PEC asteroids. Minor absorptions from 3.0 to 4.0 um indicate that [495] Eulalia's surface may contain more carbonates than [142] Polana, which potentially highlights slight differences between each family's formation and evolution. Additionally, PEC asteroids also show spectral consistency with Bennu and Ryugu, reinforcing their shared origin in the PEC. Minor absorptions in PEC asteroid spectra also connect to small bodies within and beyond the Main Belt, providing further evidence of outer Solar System formation for the PEC parent bodies.

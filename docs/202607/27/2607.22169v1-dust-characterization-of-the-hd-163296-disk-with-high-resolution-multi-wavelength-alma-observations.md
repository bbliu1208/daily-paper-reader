## 摘要
背景。行星通过原行星盘中尘埃颗粒的生长和积累而形成。表征尘埃特性，如大小、面密度和温度，是理解行星形成的关键。目的。通过对多波段高分辨率观测进行光谱能量分布（SED）拟合，表征HD 163296周围原行星盘的尘埃特性。方法。我们呈现了新的高分辨率ALMA Band 9（0.45毫米）观测，其对温度敏感。我们利用这些新的Band 9观测以及存档的ALMA Band 3、4、6和7观测，在0.066角秒的共同分辨率下进行SED拟合。我们将拟合结果与VLA观测进行比较，并探索了具有不同光学常数和孔隙度的多种尘埃模型。结果。Band 9图像显示了中心盘、两个位于0.67和1.00角秒的环，以及之前在其他波长看到的延展外缘发射。在较高频率下，环显得更宽，间隙更浅，延展发射更亮，这可以通过光学深度效应和/或大小分离来解释。我们为每个尘埃模型表征了尘埃特性，包括温度、面密度和尘埃大小。然而，推断的尘埃特性依赖于尘埃模型，仅凭ALMA数据无法确定哪个尘埃模型更优。基于VLA轮廓以及物理和观测限制，我们确定DSHARP Zubko（多孔）模型为优选模型。外环温度低于被动辐照盘模型的预测值，表明内环产生了阴影。尽管面密度和尘埃大小取决于尘埃模型，但优选模型表明中心盘、两个环以及延展盘各自包含超过几个地球质量的尘埃。

## Abstract
Context. Planets form through the growth and accumulation of dust grains in protoplanetary disks. Characterizing dust properties such as size, surface density, and temperature is key to understanding planet formation. Aims. We characterize the dust properties of the protoplanetary disk around HD 163296 by performing spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting of multi-wavelength high-resolution observations. Methods. We present new high-resolution ALMA Band 9 (0.45 mm) observations, which are sensitive to the temperature. We performed SED fitting at a common resolution of 0.066 arcsec using these new Band 9 observations along with archival ALMA Band 3, 4, 6, and 7 observations. We compared the fitted results with VLA observations and explored multiple dust models with different optical constants and porosities. Results. The Band 9 image shows the central disk, two rings at 0.67 and 1.00 arcsec, and outer extended emission previously seen at other wavelengths. At higher frequencies, the rings appear wider, the gaps appear shallower, and the extended emission appears brighter, which can be explained by optical-depth effects and/or size segregation. We characterized the dust properties, including temperature, surface density, and dust size, for each dust model. However, the inferred dust properties are dependent on the dust model, and the ALMA data alone do not allow us to determine which dust model is preferred. We identified the DSHARP Zubko (porous) model as the preferred model based on VLA profiles and physical and observational constraints. The outer ring temperature is lower than predicted by a passively irradiated disk model, suggesting shadowing by the inner ring. Although the surface density and dust size depend on the dust model, the preferred model indicates that the central disk, both rings, and the extended disk each contain more than a few Earth masses of dust.

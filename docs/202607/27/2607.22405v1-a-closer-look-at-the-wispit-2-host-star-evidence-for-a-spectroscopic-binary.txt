ABSTRACT 

Context. While hundreds of protoplanetary discs have been studied in great detail, the detection of protoplanets still embedded in their native discs remains rare. WISPIT 2 is only the second laboratory allowing for direct study of planet formation while in progress. The recently discovered system hosts two giant protoplanets in a multi-ringed disc. 

Aims. Here, we aim at characterising the WISPIT 2 host star spectroscopically to determine its stellar properties, accretion rate, and inner disc diagnostics, providing a more complete picture of the system. 

Methods. We present optical and near-infrared spectroscopic observations obtained with the ESO VLT /X-Shooter and 2.2 m /FEROS instruments. We model the stellar spectrum to determine the spectral type and e ff ective temperature, analyse the emission lines to estimate the accretion rate, and search for evidence of a close stellar companion using radial velocity measurements. 

Results. Our observations reveal that WISPIT 2 is a spectroscopic binary. The binary has a period of (4 .8 ± 0.1) days, which corresponds to a semi-major axis of 0 .072 au or 15 .54 R⊙, assuming co-planarity with the disc and a circular orbit. The binary system consists of a ∼ 0.97 M⊙ primary of spectral type K3 ( Teff ∼ 4700 K), and a ∼ 0.33 M⊙ secondary (mass ratio ∼0.34). We detect weak Hα emission, implying an accretion rate of ∼ 2 × 10 −11 M⊙ yr −1. However, this value is below the chromospheric level, suggesting little to no ongoing accretion onto the young stars. 

Conclusions. This discovery makes the WISPIT 2 disc the first circumbinary system with directly imaged protoplanets, establishing this system as a unique benchmark for studying planet formation and disc evolution around binary stars.

1. Introduction 

Thousands of mature exoplanets have now been detected, reveal-ing a remarkable diversity in planetary architectures and prop-erties that likely reflects the wide range of physical conditions present during their formation. To understand this diversity, it is fundamental to study forming protoplanets as they actively shape their natal environments. The PDS 70 system (e.g., Keppler et al. 2018) and the recently discovered WISPIT 2 system (Close et al. 2025; van Capelleveen et al. 2025; Lawlor et al. 2026) represent two confirmed cases of directly imaged protoplanetary systems. This Letter focuses on the latter. WISPIT 2 (TYC 5709-354-1) is a young ( ∼5 Myr) T Tauri system located at a distance of 

∼133 pc (van Capelleveen et al. 2025). It hosts two giant planets, WISPIT 2b and WISPIT 2c, with estimated masses of ∼4.9 and 8-10 MJup , orbiting at projected separations of ∼57 au and 15 au, respectively (van Capelleveen et al. 2025; Lawlor et al. 2026). Hα observations indicate that WISPIT 2b is actively accret-ing from the surrounding circumplanetary material at a rate of approximately 2 × 10 −12 M⊙ yr −1 (Close et al. 2025). Interest-ingly, no H α emission has been detected from the inner planet,

2. Spectroscopic observations 

We obtained two VLT /X-Shooter spectra on April 16 and May 23, 2026 (Vernet et al. 2011, DDT 116.2ASZ; PI Bürgy), and five spectra with the MPG /ESO 2.2m FEROS spectrograph (Kaufer et al. 1999) between July 9 and July 13, 2026 (DDT 117.2AS7.001; PI Benisty). Details on the observations and data reduction are given in Appendix A.1. The WISPIT 2 X-Shooter spectrum shows very few emission lines, which will be analysed in more detail (Fig. 1 and below) and is dominated by a stellar photospheric continuum (Fig. B.1). The spectrum shows a clear Li I absorption feature at 670.8 nm, confirming the young nature of the star. To characterise the spec-

3. Radial Velocity Variations and Orbital Fitting 

Between the two X-Shooter epochs, we measured a radial veloc-ity (RV) shift of 42.4 km s −1 over 37 days. To investigate the ob-served RV variation, we targeted WISPIT 2 with five additional epochs using FEROS evenly spaced by ∼ 1 day. We calculated the RVs using the cross-correlation function (CCF) technique over spectral regions free of strong telluric ab-sorption and emission lines (Tab. B.1). To derive the RV, we cross-correlated each spectrum with the best-fit stellar template from Sect. 2. A Gaussian profile was fitted to each CCF to de-termine its centroid, which provides the RV measurement, while the uncertainty was taken as the error on the centroid. This proce-dure was applied consistently to the X-Shooter and FEROS data sets. Figure C.1 shows the resulting CCFs of WISPIT 2 spanning 

∼87 days, and the corresponding RV measurements are listed in Tabs. A.1 and A.2. We modeled the RV measurements using a Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach to constrain the orbital parameters of the binary system. Specifically, we fitted a Keplerian model for a single-lined spectroscopic binary (SB1), parameterised by the orbital period ( P), time of periastron pas-sage ( T0), systemic velocity ( γ), and RV semi-amplitude ( K1).

4. Discussion & Conclusions 

4.1. WISPIT 2 orbital parameters 

The orbital fit of WISPIT 2 reveals RV variations with an ampli-tude of up to ±25 km s −1. Indeed, the consecutively taken epochs with FEROS show rapid RV variations from day to day. Our 7 RV epochs are consistent with a circular orbit, and show small residuals to the fitted model (Fig. 2). The RV fit and the TESS periodogram (Fig. 3) indicate compatible periodicity, following the binary motion over a period ∼ 4.82 days. While a circular configuration provides an excellent fit to the present data, to ro-bustly constrain the orbital parameters, including the period, ec-centricity e, and the orbit orientation ω, additional RV observa-tions spanning multiple orbital cycles will be required. 4.2. Accretion and disc evolution 

Our results indicate a very low mass accretion rate, consistent with the chromospheric noise level. In protoplanetary discs, the relationship between stellar accretion and the global properties of protoplanetary discs (such as mass and radius) provides an important diagnostic of the physical processes driving disc evo-lution (e.g., Lodato et al. 2017; Manara et al. 2023). In systems hosting massive planets, however, the accretion flow can also be strongly modified by planet-disc interactions, which regu-late the transport of gas through the disc toward the central star. Following Manara et al. (2019), we compare the observed accretion rates and disc masses with the predictions of plane-tary population synthesis models for a ∼2 Myr-old disc popu-lation hosting giant planets (Mordasini et al. 2009, 2012) . In Fig. 4, we show these predictions together with observations of transition discs in various star-forming regions (Manara et al. 2023; Pinilla et al. 2018). We additionally mark the positions of PDS 70 and WISPIT 2, adopting the dust-continuum-based disc mass estimate as lower limit (Facchini et al. 2026). Both systems occupy a similar region of the ˙Macc –Mdisc plane, where their low accretion rates are consistent with the expected influence of giant

planets that block material beyond their orbits and starve the in-ner disc regions. The lack of significant H α variability provides further evidence that the transport of material from the outer disc is strongly inhibited by the giant planets, limiting the amount of material that can reach the star. This is consistent with the ob-served gas- and dust-depleted cavity in 12 CO J = 3 − 2 within the orbit of WISPIT 2c (Benisty et al. in prep). While the accretion properties of WISPIT 2 are similar to those of PDS 70, the two systems di ff er in their wind signatures. PDS 70 shows the forbidden [OI] 6300 Å emission line, evidence for a magnetically driven disc wind launched close to the star (Campbell-White et al. 2023; Gaidos et al. 2024). This emission is not detected in WISPIT 2, suggesting that any disc wind is either absent or significantly weaker. 

4.3. Circumbinary discs and planets 

Binary stars are ubiquitous, with ∼65% of stars forming in multiple systems (Duchêne & Kraus 2013; O ff ner et al. 2023). In circumbinary discs, the central binary clears a cavity ex-tending to ∼2-3 times the binary separation (Cuello et al. 2025; Artymowicz & Lubow 1994). This cavity can in turn re-duce the e ffi ciency of mass transport onto the stars, poten-tially allowing circumbinary discs to retain more mass and remain larger at a given age compared to discs around sin-gle stars (Alexander 2012; Ronco et al. 2021). In this context, WISPIT 2 is particularly reminiscent of V4046 Sgr, a well-studied circumbinary system with prominent and highly struc-tured disc (Galloway-Sprietsma et al. 2025), at an advanced evo-lutionary stage ( ∼20 Myr) compared to typical disc lifetime (Hernández et al. 2007). The presence of the binary might be key to the long-lived nature of the disc, potentially providing a more favourable environment for the formation and survival of such massive, wide-orbit planets (e.g., Ruíz-Rodríguez et al. 2019). The population of circumbinary (P-type) planets around ma-ture stars remains small, with only 46 planets known in 37 sys-tems 2. Most are transit detections and occupy compact config-urations, orbiting just beyond the tidally truncated inner edge of the circumbinary disc consistent with inward migration be-ing halted at the cavity edge. Only a handful of circumbinary planets are known beyond 5 au, and just two systems share simi-larities with WISPIT 2: HD 155555 hosts a 3.8 M Jup companion at 7.3 au around a 1.68-day binary (Gratton et al. 2024), while HD 143811 hosts a ∼6 M Jup planet at ∼64 au around an 18.6-day binary (Squicciarini et al. 2025). With its 4.7-day binary and planets at ∼15 and ∼58 au, WISPIT 2 belongs to this very small class of wide-orbit planets around compact binaries, but is so far the only one caught at the time of formation. 

5. Conclusions 

In this Letter, we have shown, using VLT /X-Shooter and FEROS spectroscopy, that WISPIT 2 is a spectroscopic binary with a pe-riod of 4 .8 ± 0.1 days, consisting of a K3 ∼0.97 M⊙ primary and a ∼ 0.33 M⊙ secondary, with little to no ongoing accretion. WISPIT 2 becomes the first system in which two giant proto-planets have been directly imaged within a circumbinary disc and provides a key benchmark for studying planet formation and disc evolution around binary stars.

Appendix A: Observational Information 

A.1. Observations and data reduction 

The two epochs of X-Shooter spectra were observed within the DDT program 116.2ASZ (PI Bürgy). The first spectrum was taken on April 16, 2026 at airmass-corrected seeing ∼ 1.3”, above the requested < 1”. Therefore, the observations were repeated on May 23, 2026 and carried out at airmass-corrected seeing ∼ 1”, as requested. During the first observations sky conditions were "Clear", while in the second epoch "Photometric". Both spectra were obtained with a narrow slit for the highest available spectral resolution and a wide slit for absolute flux calibration. The narrow slit observations were obtained using 0.5", 0.4", and 0.4" wide slits for the UVB, VIS, and NIR arms, respectively. Observations were performed in ABBA nodding mode to remove background sky emission. The wide slit observations were performed in stare mode using the 5" slit at the beginning of the observations. The corresponding reported spectral resolution of this observing setup is R ∼ 9700, 18400, and 11600 for the three arms, respectively. The X-Shooter data was reduced using the X-Shooter data reduction pipeline (v. 3.8.3; Modigliani et al. 2010), within the EsoReflex workflow (v. 2.11.5; Freudling et al. 2013), at default settings with the exception of the 1D spectral extraction window which was adapted to exclude a second star located within the slit for one of the nodding positions. Subsequently, the reduced spectra of the VIS and NIR arm were corrected for telluric absorption using Molecfit (v. 4.4.4.82 Smette et al. 2015; Kausch et al. 2015). The resulting spectra were re-scaled in flux to match the spectra taken with the wide slit in order to correct for flux losses. Finally, the spectra were individually corrected for the Earth’s motion relative to the local standard of rest (LSRK) at the time of observation. The FEROS observations were carried out under the DDT program 117.2AS7.001 (PI Benisty) between July 9 and July 13 2026, with an exposure time of 900 s each. The spectra were reduced using the ceres reduction pipeline 3 for echelle spectrographs (Brahm et al. 2017).
